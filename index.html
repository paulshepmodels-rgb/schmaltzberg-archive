ARCHIVE NOTE

The following file concerns two anomalous Dalek units believed to originate from an abandoned reconnaissance programme conducted prior to a proposed Dalek incursion into the Discworld.

The precise circumstances remain uncertain.

What is known is that the Daleks made a serious intelligence error.

A remarkably serious intelligence error.

TORCHWOOD ASSESSMENT

The two known Schmaltzberg Daleks represent an unusual intersection of Dalek technology, Discworld culture and one of the most significant intelligence failures in recorded inter-dimensional history.

They are not merely smaller Daleks. They are physical evidence of a strategic misunderstanding on a potentially planetary scale. The machines were designed to fit a world that the Daleks believed was populated almost entirely by Dwarfs.

It was not. Fortunately for everyone involved, nobody appears to have told them.

UNIT DOSSIER: CASANUNDA

A compact reconnaissance Dalek bearing the designation Casanunda.

The name is believed to have been acquired during contact with the Dwarfs of Schmaltzberg. Records indicate that the unit demonstrated unusual persistence when navigating complex underground environments.

Whether the name was assigned by the Daleks, adopted from a local individual, or resulted from a catastrophic translation error is unknown.

Torchwood analysts have noted that the name corresponds to a well-known Dwarfish figure associated with the Discworld. The Daleks appear to have regarded this coincidence as entirely unremarkable.

UNIT DOSSIER: STRONGINTHEARM

The second known unit carries the designation Stronginthearm. As with Casanunda, the name appears to have originated from the Dwarfish population encountered during the reconnaissance operation.

Stronginthearm appears to have been intended as a development of the original compact scout platform, incorporating lessons learned during underground operations.

The unit's exact operational history is unknown. It is possible that Stronginthearm was produced after the Daleks realised that the Discworld's subterranean environment was considerably more extensive than first believed.

It is equally possible that nobody informed Davros that most of the Discworld was not, in fact, inhabited by Dwarfs.

THE RECONNAISSANCE

Prior to the planned invasion of the Discworld, a Dalek reconnaissance force entered through a region subsequently identified as Schmaltzberg. There they encountered an extensive population of Dwarfs and a vast network of tunnels, mines and passages.

  1. The inhabitants were considerably shorter than anticipated.
  2. The majority of accessible passages were significantly narrower than standard Dalek dimensions.
  3. The tunnel network appeared to extend far beyond the limits initially surveyed.

THE FUNDAMENTAL ERROR

The Daleks concluded that the inhabitants encountered at Schmaltzberg were representative of the Discworld population:

THE INHABITANTS ARE SMALL.
THE TUNNELS ARE SMALL.
THEREFORE THE PLANET IS SMALL.

Strategic Assessment: “FULL-SIZE DALEKS MAY BE UNABLE TO ACCESS SIGNIFICANT AREAS OF THE PLANET.”

THE SCHMALTZBERG PROJECT

Davros authorised a compact reconnaissance platform at 60% of standard Dalek scale, offering improved access through confined spaces, reduced logistics, greater manoeuvrability underground, and the psychological advantage of appearing unexpectedly small.

THE INVASION THAT NEVER HAPPENED

Invasion command suffered logistical difficulties attempting to determine how an entire planet could fit inside a network of tunnels. A proposal to manufacture an even smaller invasion force was rejected:

“EXTERMINATING TINY PEOPLE IS NOT AN EFFICIENT USE OF DALEK RESOURCES.”

Torchwood has been unable to determine whether this statement was genuine or sarcastic. The latter possibility is considered extremely unlikely.

META FILE // REAL-WORLD PROP HISTORY & BUILD LOG

1988 SCREEN ORIGINS: REMEMBRANCE OF THE DALEKS

1988 REFERENCE PHOTO: imperial1988.jpg
1988 BBC IMPERIAL DALEK // TAP TO ZOOM

Both props trace their lineage to the 1988 Seventh Doctor serial Remembrance of the Daleks—the final Dalek story of the classic series and the very first Dalek story the builder remembers watching as a child.

Set in 1963 London, the serial staged a Dalek civil war between the grey-and-black Renegade faction and Davros’s cream-white and gold Imperial Daleks.

To portray the four Imperial props, the BBC Visual Effects team cast brand-new fibreglass shells from a modified Revelation of the Daleks shoulder plug—building in raised mesh edges, slat blocks, and solid fibreglass neck drums for crisp, repeatable production.

HISTORICAL ARCHIVE REFERENCE: Detailed histories of the 1988 screen props (and Mike Tucker’s splitting Imperial Emperor) are documented at dalek6388.co.uk/remembrance-of-the-daleks/

BUILDING THE SCHMALTZBERG DALEKS (60% → 62.4%)

With limited workshop space and printer capacity, 60% scale was chosen for a very practical reason: it was the maximum size the dome could be 3D-printed as a single complete part on an Ender 5 Plus.

PRINTERCreality Ender 5 Plus (single-piece dome limit)
DEBUTSci-Fi Scarborough — April 2026 (Casanunda)
ELECTRONICSColour-changing dome lights + custom Raspberry Pi voice modulator

The Real-World Miscalculation: Dalek Casanunda debuted at Sci-Fi Scarborough in April 2026 with classic maiden-outing teething issues (and no lights or voice modulator yet). Studying the convention photos afterward revealed that something looked subtly off—and just like the Daleks miscalculating the size of the Discworld, a scaling error was discovered.

THE SHOULDER SECTION (THE ONE PART NOT DRAWN IN CAD BY HAND) HAD ACTUALLY BEEN SCALED TO 62.4% — NOT 60%.

“Trigger’s Broom” & Birth of Stronginthearm: Over the 2026 season, Casanunda’s skirt and upper sections were progressively reprinted at 62.4% to match the shoulders—leaving behind enough original components to joke about building a classic “Trigger’s Broom” out of spare Dalek parts.

Instead, printing one extra 62.4% shoulder section gave birth to Dalek Stronginthearm. Because Stronginthearm’s leftover proportions weren’t strictly 1988 screen-accurate, the prop became an intentional crossover between Remembrance Imperials and modern Time War Daleks—complete with a New-Series eye, eyestalk cowl, and plunger.

Today, both units feature colour-changing dome lights synchronised to a custom Raspberry Pi voice modulator designed by the builder.
